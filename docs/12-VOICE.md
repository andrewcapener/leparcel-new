# 12 · Voice

How Mermade Market sounds anywhere words render: the site, the form, the emails,
the signage. The register shifts between shopper pages and vendor/admin pages
(see 08-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md §5), but the voice underneath is one voice.

## The idea

Concise, and warm the way a good host is warm: by paying attention, not by
saying so. There is a real sense of community here and of everyone having fun
together, and we never say either of those things on the nose. They are implied
by detail and by how we treat people, or they are absent.

So: "Come early, bring a bag, and talk to people" carries the whole feeling.
"Join our vibrant community of makers" would kill it.

## Rules

1. **Short sentences.** Cut until it hurts, then put one concrete detail back.
2. **No em dashes, no en dashes. Ever.** Use a period, a comma, a colon, or a
   parenthesis; ranges take a plain hyphen ("$18-$240", "November 13-15",
   "5-9pm"). The only exceptions are the signature line of an email
   ("— Mermade Market") and the typographic placeholder for an empty table
   cell.
3. **Specifics carry the warmth.** "Friday evening is the calm one." "One
   register at the front means one bag at the end." Adjectives don't; details do.
4. **Numbers are plain and sourced.** Never publish a count or a superlative
   nobody can back (09-CONTENT-AUDIT.md §5).
5. **Promises are operational.** "We read every application and answer either
   way." Never aspirational, never "we strive to."
6. **Talk to one person.** "You" is singular: one shopper, one maker.
7. **"We" is the people in the room**, makers included. Not a company.
8. **No exclamation points.** Showing up twice a year for eleven years is the
   enthusiasm.
9. **Vendor and admin copy stays precise and unambiguous.** Warmth is fine;
   ambiguity about money, dates, or duties never is.

## Vocabulary

Use: makers, the room, the floor, the list, the show, load-in, the register.

Avoid: community, vibrant, curated experience, elevate, artisanal (as an
adjective; "a juried market of independent makers" is the noun phrase), "join
us!", and anything a mall's marketing team would write.

## Test

Read it aloud. If it sounds like a person who runs the show and knows your name,
ship it. If it sounds like a website, cut it in half and try again.
