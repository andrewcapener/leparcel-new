# What the rest of the field actually says

**Research backing `10-VENDOR-AGREEMENT.md` · August 25, 2026**

Every comparative claim in the draft agreement traces to something here. Quotes
are verbatim from the sources cited unless marked otherwise.

---

## 0. The headline finding

**Nobody in the American craft-fair world runs your indoor model with published
terms.** Checked: Renegade Craft, West Coast Craft, Patchwork Show, One of a
Kind (Chicago/Toronto), Crafty Wonderland, Urban Craft Uprising, American Craft
Council, Rose Bowl Flea, Junk Bonanza, Field + Supply, Unique Markets, Vintage
Market Days, Bazaar Bizarre, and the Junior League holiday markets in
Indianapolis, Tampa and Montgomery.

Every one of them is booth-rental. The Junior League markets were the best
hypothesis — nonprofit, volunteer-staffed, exactly the profile where central
checkout would make sense — and JL Indianapolis answers it flatly: *"No. Merchant
participation is a flat booth fee only."* The Smithsonian Craft Show markets the
absence of commission as a *feature*: *"does not charge sales commissions."*

The closest thing to your commission mechanic anywhere is **food vendors**:
Renegade takes a percentage of self-reported food-truck gross, and Urban Craft
Uprising's Seattle Parks food arrangement is *"20% of your sales in total."*
Neither involves the organiser holding the register.

**Consequence:** Schedule B (outdoor) can be benchmarked against a dozen peers.
Schedule A (indoor) has to be built from consignment law and retail-consignment
practice, because there is no craft-market precedent to copy. That is where the
legal risk concentrates and where the lawyer hour should go.

## 1. Cancellation windows

| Market | Window | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Patchwork Show** | 30 days | *"If you need to cancel you must do so thirty days before the show for a refund of your booth fee (application fees cannot be refunded). If you cancel after that, you forfeit your booth fee."* |
| **West Coast Craft** | 30 days | *"If a space is cancelled by a Vendor up to 30 days before the first day of the event weekend, the Vendor will receive a full refund minus the application fee."* Inside 30 days: *"no payment or part of payment will be refunded."* |
| **Urban Craft Uprising** | 6 weeks | Full refund at 6 weeks; *"there is a $50 penalty in addition to forfeiture of you booth fee, for any vendor dropouts that happen within four weeks of the show."* |
| **Crafty Wonderland** | Tiered | Free until the payment deadline; *"refunded minus a $50 cancellation fee"* until Oct 31; *"will not be refunded under any circumstance"* after Nov 1 |
| **One of a Kind** | 90 days | *"Any exhibitor who cancels less than 90 days prior to the start of the show will be responsible for 100% of the amount of the contract."* |
| **Rose Bowl Flea** | None | *"NO REFUNDS, CREDITS OR EXCHANGES." "ALL SALES ARE FINAL."* |
| **Mermade today** | None | *"all spaces nonrefundable"* |

**Read:** 30 days is the SoCal norm and the two markets closest to you in size and
audience both use it. Application/jury fees are non-refundable everywhere, without
exception.

## 2. Weather and force majeure — where you cannot copy anyone

Every market that addresses weather puts 100% of the risk on the vendor:

- **Renegade:** *"The Event(s) will proceed regardless of the weather."* And if
  Renegade itself cancels: *"the Event will not be rescheduled and there will be
  no refunds to Participant."*
- **Patchwork:** *"Patchwork Show is a rain or shine event and we do not refund
  vendors due to unseasonable weather."*
- **Rose Bowl:** *"EVENT GOES ON RAIN OR SHINE."*
- **One of a Kind** has the deepest drafting — a fully lawyered, COVID-tested
  force majeure definition running to a paragraph, plus a unilateral venue-change
  right, all with *"the 'Manager' shall not be liable to Exhibitor for any loss."*

**You postponed Fall 2025 for rain.** A rain-or-shine clause is therefore a term
you have already broken once, and it would be the first thing an unhappy vendor
pointed at. This is the one place in the agreement where the industry standard is
actively wrong for you and the draft departs from it deliberately.

## 3. Insurance

| Market | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **One of a Kind** | *"$5,000,000.00, per occurrence"*, naming manager + venue + all service groups as additional insured, *"thirty (30) days notice of cancellation."* Food vendors need a separate $5M policy filed three weeks out |
| **West Coast Craft** | GL $1M occurrence / $2M aggregate; damage to rented premises $100K; medical $5K; personal & advertising injury $1M; products/completed ops $2M |
| **Renegade** | Representation only, no limit: *"Participant represents that it has, or will have at the time of the Event, appropriate insurance"* |
| **Crafty Wonderland** | Alcohol vendors only |
| **UCU, Patchwork, Rose Bowl, Unique Markets, ACC, Junk Bonanza, Field + Supply** | Nothing published |

**Read:** The $1M/$2M figure in the draft is West Coast Craft's, because they are
the closest peer that publishes real numbers. $5M is trade-show territory and
would be absurd to ask a candle maker for. Note that most of the indie tier
requires nothing at all publicly — so requiring a COI puts you *above* the field,
which supports the blanket-policy option in §7 as a differentiator rather than a
burden.

## 4. Indemnity — the most universal clause in the field

Every organisation that publishes any legal terms has one, and every one runs in
a single direction. Even **Urban Craft Uprising**, which publishes almost nothing
else, tells applicants *"you will also be required to sign a hold harmless
agreement."*

- **Renegade** runs two separate indemnities — one for legal compliance, one for
  liability: *"Participant will indemnify, defend, and hold RCF (and its
  employees, representatives, contractors, vendors, affiliates, and
  representatives) harmless against any claims related to Participant's
  liabilities, actions, or omissions."*
- **One of a Kind** extends it to the venue and every service supplier, expressly
  covering *"loss or damage to the 'Exhibitor's' property caused by Fire, Theft,
  Accident or any cause whatsoever during the moving of materials."*
- **Informa** (One of a Kind's parent) adds a separate IP-infringement indemnity
  for *"the display of any products, documents or other materials exhibited."*

---

## 5. Liability disclaimers — near-boilerplate

The construction *"we provide security but are not liable for theft"* recurs
almost verbatim across independently drafted agreements:

- **One of a Kind:** *"'Manager' provides general building security for the
  duration of the show... however 'Manager' is not responsible for any theft, loss
  of material or damage to exhibitor property."*
- **Renegade:** *"Participant expressly assumes all risks of loss, damage,
  liability, injury, or destruction resulting from any cause whatsoever."*
- **O'Connell Center (UF):** *"Security guards will be on duty overnight, but the
  University cannot guarantee the safety of materials."*
- **Informa** caps everything: *"maximum aggregate liability... shall be limited
  to the total amount of the Fees."*

**But none of these markets take possession of the goods.** That is why Schedule
A §A8 departs from this pattern — a blanket disclaimer makes sense when the
vendor is standing next to their own table, and much less sense when you carried
their inventory into a room they aren't allowed in.

## 6. Handmade rules and the new AI clause

- **Crafty Wonderland:** *"The items represented for sale MUST BE HANDMADE OR HAND
  ALTERED BY YOU! No mass-produced items, imported goods, or multi-level marketing
  companies will be allowed... WE DO NOT ALLOW ANY AI GENERATED ART TO BE SOLD AT
  OUR MARKETS!"*
- **Patchwork Show:** *"We DON'T accept vendors who resell products that are not
  designed by them Ie: things like Mary Kay or Scentsy candles... Products with AI
  generated images are also prohibited."*
- **American Craft Council** — strongest enforcement language found: *"Submitted
  work must be small-studio production made under the direct supervision of the
  applicant,"* and *"ACC reserves the right to require authentication of the
  production process at any time by requesting documentary evidence."*
- **One of a Kind** ties it to a contractual item list: *"ONLY ITEMS ACCEPTED BY
  THE SELECTION COMMITTEE SHALL BE PERMITTED TO BE SOLD AT THE SHOW."*
- **Urban Craft Uprising:** vendors may sell only *"the specific product lines and
  craft category that you applied with."*

**Read:** The AI ban is new, spreading fast, and two markets adopted nearly
identical wording independently. Your application already asks the question — the
clause is what makes the answer mean something.

## 7. Booth sharing

Universal that it must be pre-cleared through jurying; split on mechanics.

- **Crafty Wonderland:** *"limiting it to only two vendors per booth... both
  vendors need to apply together on the same application and will be juried as a
  pair."*
- **Urban Craft Uprising:** *"You will each need to fill out the application
  separately, and will be juried separately."* — the exact opposite mechanic.
- **Renegade:** *"Participant may not assign this Agreement, or Participant's
  space at an Event, to any third party... Only the accepted Participants are able
  to sell or distribute their items."*
- **One of a Kind:** *"The 'Exhibitor' CANNOT assign, share, split, and/or sell
  either the whole or any part of the rights and space granted to them."*

## 8. Photography

Only the two most contractually mature organisations publish anything, and they
diverge sharply.

**Renegade** — the model the draft follows, and the only one that promises credit:

> *"Participant hereby irrevocably grants RCF the right to post and distribute
> such Event-related photographs and/or videos, in any format or channel now known
> or yet to be developed... If Participant has provided RCF with Participant's
> social media handle(s), RCF will make reasonable efforts to tag Participant."*

**Informa / One of a Kind** — the model the draft deliberately avoids:

> *"Informa Group is the exclusive owner of all rights in the Content and hereby
> waives any and all... rights in and to such Content... Informa Group shall be
> permitted to use the Content anywhere in the world for promotional and all other
> purposes, without any payment or compensation."*

Combined, in the same document, with: *"All unauthorised photography and the
recording or transmitting of audio or visual material... is expressly
prohibited."* Unlimited rights for them, none for the vendor.

## 9. Permits and sales tax

- **Rose Bowl:** *"A valid California Seller's Permit is required to sell in the
  Stadium – Blue/Gold Area."* Plus the actual exemption form by name:
  *"Occasional sellers may qualify with the required CDTFA-410-D form."*
- **West Coast Craft:** *"All vendors must bring a CA Seller's Permit"* — note
  *bring*, i.e. to the show, not at application.
- **Patchwork:** tells vendors they must hold one, then disclaims monitoring.
- **Urban Craft Uprising** — the only vendor-friendly accommodation found:
  *"you are allowed TWO courtesy waivers each year, to attend shows without a
  license."*

**Read:** this confirms yesterday's correction. Where permits are collected at
all, they are collected at or near the event, never as a gate on the application.

## 10. No-shows and early teardown

- **One of a Kind** — the only market that names it as a breach: *"An unattended
  booth will be considered to be in breach of this contract,"* and *"The
  'Exhibitor' undertakes to occupy the Space in time for the opening of the Event
  and not to close the Exhibition Stand prior to the end of the Event."* Tied to
  forfeiture *"as liquidated damages and not as penalty."*
- **ACC:** *"Exhibitors must be present for the duration of the event. Exhibitors'
  employees or sales representatives may not substitute for the exhibitor."*
- **O'Connell Center:** staffed *"by individuals over the age of 18 until show
  close on both days."*

Everywhere else it is left to general forfeiture, which in practice means nothing
happens.

## 11. The California consignment law that governs Schedule A

### Retailer of record — the answer to your open tax question

**Cal. Code Regs. tit. 18 §1569**, verbatim:

> *"A person who has possession of property owned by another, and also the power
> to cause title to that property to be transferred to a third person without any
> further action on the part of its owner, and who exercises such power, is a
> retailer when the party to whom title is transferred is a consumer."*

**CDTFA Publication 114 (Consignment Sales)** restates it as a two-part test — you
are the retailer when you *"have possession or control of the item you are
selling"* and *"can transfer ownership or use of the item to the buyer without
further action on the part of the owner"* — and uses a jewelry store selling
consigned jewelry as the worked example.

Indoor Mermade meets both prongs. **Mermade is the retailer; sales tax is on the
full retail price; indoor makers need no permit for your sales.** Publication 111,
by contrast, governs the outdoor track, where vendors sell for themselves.

### Fine art consignment — probably not you, but check

**Cal. Civ. Code §1738 et seq.** creates agency, a trust over the goods exempt
from the consignee's creditors, a trust over proceeds, and a non-waivable duty of
care (§1738.8: *"Any provision of a contract or agreement whereby the consignor
waives any provision of this title is void"*).

It only reaches *"fine art,"* defined as *"a painting, sculpture, drawing, work of
graphic art (including an etching, lithograph, offset print, silk screen, or a
work of graphic art of like nature), a work of calligraphy, or a work in mixed
media."* That is a closed list. Jewelry, textiles, candles, leather, functional
ceramics sit outside it. A hand-pulled print or a sculptural ceramic piece
plausibly sits inside.

Schedule A adopts the trust structure voluntarily for everything, because it may
apply to some pieces by operation of law regardless, and because replicating it
costs you nothing you should not already be doing.

### The creditor problem — the real structural risk

**Cal. Com. Code §9319(a):** while goods are in the consignee's possession, *"the
consignee is deemed to have rights and title to the goods identical to those the
consignor had or had power to transfer."* Unless perfected by a UCC-1 filing,
consigned goods can be reached by the consignee's creditors, including a
bankruptcy trustee's strong-arm powers.

And Article 9's consignment definition may not even reach you: §9-102(a)(20)
excludes a consignee *"generally known by its creditors to be substantially
engaged in selling the goods of others"* — which an eleven-year-old market with a
public consignment model may well be. If Article 9 does not apply, **Cal. Com.
Code §2326(2)** gets to the same place by another road: goods held on sale or
return *"are subject to such claims while in the buyer's possession."*

Every path leads to the same exposure. No maker is filing an individual UCC-1 for
a weekend's candles. **Ask counsel about an operator-level filing.**

### 1099s

IRS instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC exclude *"[p]ayments for
merchandise, telegrams, telephone, freight, storage, and similar items."* A
consignment payout is a merchandise payment, not compensation for services, which
is why consignment shops generally do not 1099 consignors. Confirm with your CPA.
2026 thresholds: 1099-NEC/MISC raised to **$2,000**; 1099-K back to **$20,000 and
200 transactions**.

### Commercial benchmarks

- **Commission:** galleries commonly 80/20 or 60/40 to the artist; general resale
  consignment 40–60% to the consignor. **Your 80/20 is at the generous end** —
  closer to fine-art gallery terms than to resale-shop terms.
- **Payout timing:** *"within thirty (30) days after the close"* is the event
  benchmark.
- **Risk of loss:** resale shops disclaim broadly — Consignment Classics: *"Fire
  and theft insurance is the responsibility of the consignor"*; Modo Boutique
  disclaims *"theft or damage... customer handling, fire, flood, earthquakes."*
  They hold goods for months, unattended. You hold them for a weekend, staffed.
- **Abandonment:** 90–120 days then the shop keeps them, in retail consignment.
  For a weekend event the analogue is days, and converting a maker's work into
  your own inventory is a different act from a thrift store doing it.
